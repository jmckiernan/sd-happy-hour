# Launch Strategy

## Objective

Launch San Diego as a local demand network, not just a directory. The product should prove that consumers come back, share lists, subscribe to alerts, and help create merchant demand.

## Launch Thesis

The white paper's key insight is that consumers can get value before merchants fully participate. That means the launch should optimize for density, trust, and repeat usage before scaling outward.

## Audience Segments

| Segment | What they need | What to ask them to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consumers | Fast answers on where to go now | Browse, save, follow, share, subscribe |
| Founding merchants | A reason to claim and upgrade | Join beta, test promotions, share results |
| Local creators | Content and audience value | Publish lists, guides, and neighborhood picks |
| Friends and family network | Simple social utility | Invite others, share lists, spread the word |

## Launch Messaging

1. Consumer message: find the best happy hours, deals, and things happening in San Diego right now.
2. Merchant message: fill seats when you need them filled.
3. Internal message: build the real-time local demand network.

## Launch Channels

### 1. Founder-Led Distribution

This is the first and most important channel. The white paper is explicit that the founder network is a launch asset, not an afterthought.

Actions:

1. Invite close contacts into the app early.
2. Ask them to follow venues, save lists, and share deals.
3. Recruit known local restaurant operators as design partners.
4. Use direct outreach before paid acquisition.

### 2. SEO And Editorial Content

Content should be a repeatable acquisition engine.

Actions:

1. Publish neighborhood and occasion-based guides.
2. Tie every article back to live venue pages and lists.
3. Reuse each research piece across blog, social, and email.
4. Build pages that answer high-intent searches like "best happy hours near me" and "things to do tonight in San Diego."

### 3. Social Content

Content should feel local, useful, and shareable rather than generic marketing.

Actions:

1. Post short-form neighborhood roundups.
2. Highlight time-sensitive offers.
3. Repackage blog research into carousels and short videos.
4. Use creators with strong local engagement rather than chasing only large accounts.

### 4. Merchant Distribution

Merchants can become their own distribution channel.

Actions:

1. Give venues a way to claim listings and promote them.
2. Make it easy to share a venue page or campaign.
3. Show merchants how many followers and alerts they have.
4. Turn early merchants into case studies.

### 5. User Referrals

Sharing is the virality loop.

Actions:

1. One share button everywhere.
2. Shareable lists and deal pages.
3. Invite friends to collaborative lists.
4. Referral tracking on shared links.

## Launch Phases

### Prelaunch

1. Fill the data set with enough San Diego venues to feel useful.
2. Build the first sharing, alert, and list flows.
3. Prepare founding merchant beta offers.
4. Draft the first content cluster and social templates.

### Soft Launch

1. Invite a small, concentrated audience.
2. Measure whether people save places, share lists, and subscribe to alerts.
3. Test merchant upgrade language and campaign flows.
4. Watch for data gaps and user confusion.

### Public Launch

1. Publish the first content engine cluster.
2. Announce the product through founder and local networks.
3. Activate creators and local communities.
4. Start telling the merchant story with real audience numbers.

### Postlaunch

1. Watch retention and sharing, not just traffic.
2. Reinvest in the channels that produce active users.
3. Use merchant case studies as sales collateral.
4. Expand neighborhood by neighborhood before expanding too broadly.

## Operating Rhythm

Weekly questions:

1. What did we ship?
2. What drove new users?
3. What caused people to share?
4. What made merchants act?
5. What did we learn from the data?

Monthly review:

1. Which neighborhoods are dense enough to matter?
2. Which content formats convert best?
3. Which merchant pitch is working?
4. Which alert preferences are most used?
5. Which channels bring retained users instead of one-time visits?

## Launch Risks

1. Launching too broadly before San Diego density exists.
2. Spending on paid media before the organic message is validated.
3. Treating merchant monetization as the first step instead of the proof step.
4. Overbuilding the analytics dashboard before enough behavior exists to report meaningfully.
5. Letting content become generic SEO instead of local utility.
