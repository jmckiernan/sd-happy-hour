# Launch Timeline

## Assumptions

This timeline assumes work starts from the current product state and that the team wants a staged rollout rather than a big-bang launch. If you want, this can later be converted into calendar dates.

## Timeline

| Window | Focus | Deliverables | Exit Criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks 1-2 | Foundation planning | Finalize roadmap, data model, and metrics; confirm merchant pricing assumptions; define alert families and share flows | Product scope is locked enough to build against |
| Weeks 3-5 | Consumer loop | Shared lists, share buttons, alert preferences, quiet hours, pause toggle, list collaboration | Users can share, save, follow, and subscribe cleanly |
| Weeks 6-8 | Merchant loop | Upgrade page, billing provider integration plan, campaign gating, live promotion CTA handling | A merchant hitting the free limit sees a clear upgrade path |
| Weeks 9-10 | Analytics foundation | First-party event tracking, merchant dashboard skeleton, share and alert attribution | Core actions can be measured end to end |
| Weeks 11-12 | Content engine | Neighborhood guides, deal roundups, social repurposing workflow, SEO landing pages | The site starts acquiring users from content |
| Weeks 13-14 | Beta launch | Founding merchants, pilot users, controlled release, feedback loop | Enough usage to validate the loop and fix gaps |
| Weeks 15-18 | Public launch | Wider San Diego push, creator collaborations, social campaigns, referral mechanics | The app has repeatable acquisition and engagement |
| Months 5-8 | Monetization expansion | Paid plans, boost concepts, richer analytics, merchant case studies | The first merchants see enough value to keep paying |
| Months 9-12 | Scale prep | Category expansion, stronger ingestion pipeline, new neighborhood templates, new market playbook | The launch system can be reused elsewhere |

## Critical Dependencies

1. A stable venue and promotion data model.
2. A clear merchant upgrade path.
3. Shareable pages with good previews.
4. User-facing alert preferences that are easy to understand.
5. A reporting layer that can show real activity to merchants.

## Suggested Milestones

### Milestone 1

The app can share any venue or list and support user-controlled alerts.

### Milestone 2

A merchant can pay or upgrade after the free campaign limit is reached.

### Milestone 3

The merchant dashboard can show early proof of value.

### Milestone 4

Content and sharing start producing measurable organic growth.

### Milestone 5

The same operating model can be repeated in another neighborhood or city.
